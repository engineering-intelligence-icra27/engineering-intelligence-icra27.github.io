01 / The workshop

Workshop overview

Traditional engineering workflows distribute design, manufacturing, and robotic execution across specialized tools and sequential stages. Yet product geometry determines both functional performance and how a product can be manufactured, grasped, assembled, and inspected. Manufacturing processes and robotic capabilities, in turn, constrain feasible designs. These dependencies create an opportunity to jointly optimize products, production processes, and robotic behavior, using feedback from simulation, fabrication, and physical interaction.

Progress in generative CAD, language-guided robotics, engineering benchmarks, and robot co-design makes this timely. The workshop examines joint design–process optimization and agentic engineering workflows that coordinate CAD/CAE/CAM, optimization, and robotic execution. How should coupled decisions be represented? When does joint optimization outperform sequential workflows? How can manufacturing feedback improve subsequent designs?

Applications span component geometry and fabrication processes, robotic assembly, and hardware–policy co-design.

03 / Research directions

Across the entire
engineering loop.

Two complementary themes.
One focus on learning across stages.

I

Joint optimization of designs,
processes, and operation

Coupling geometry, materials, fabrication, and behavior—from component design and manufacturing constraints to robot hardware–policy co-design.

Generative designFabrication constraintsHardware co-design
Where does joint optimization matter?+
  1. Representations and constraints. Should fabrication constraints enter the design representation, the objective, or a learned feasibility model?
  2. Joint versus modular optimization. When do coupled decisions outperform sequential workflows, especially with competing objectives and expensive evaluations?
  3. Physics and feedback. Can simulation, fabrication, and inspection close the gap between as-designed and as-built performance?
II

Agentic workflows across engineering design and manufacturing

Coordinating CAD/CAE/CAM, simulation, optimization, and robotic execution—and using measurements and expert corrections to revise decisions.

Tool-using agentsRobotic executionPhysical evaluation
How should agents coordinate engineering?+
  1. Coordination and revision. Which decisions belong with optimization tools, learned models, robot skills, or human experts?
  2. Learning across stages. How can tool interactions, fabrication failures, and inspection results improve subsequent designs and processes?
  3. Complete-workflow evaluation. What evidence establishes improvements in quality, manufacturability, robotic success, cost, and expert effort?

↳ From simulation to fabrication to physical interaction—and back to design.
